Peer-Reviewed Publications

  1. NeurIPS 2026
    AudioSphere: Towards Self-Supervised Spatial Audio Representation Models
    Göksenin Yüksel, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026
    Abs Bib Code

    Recent audio embedding models learn general-purpose representations that transfer across clip- and frame-level tasks, yet they operate almost exclusively on single-channel signals and are blind to spatial structure. We introduce AudioSphere, a multi-channel masked autoencoder trained on Ambisonics recordings that reconstructs both spectral content and directional cues via a joint spatiotemporal masking strategy. Alongside the model, we release RealSELD, a standardized benchmark for evaluating frozen backbones on real-world multi-channel sound event localization and detection (SELD). AudioSphere enables, for the first time, SELD with a frozen self-supervised backbone, while matching or exceeding prior general-purpose audio embedding models on the HEAR benchmark.

  3. MIDL
    Style Randomization Improves the Robustness of Breast Density Estimation in MR Images
    Göksenin Yüksel, Koen Eppenhof, Jaap Kroes, and Marcel Worring
    In Proceedings of the 7th International Conference on Medical Imaging with Deep Learning (MIDL), 2024
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Breast density, a crucial risk factor for future breast cancer development, is defined by the ratio of fat to fibro-glandular tissue (FGT) in the breast. Accurate breast and FGT segmentation is essential for robust density estimation. We propose a novel data augmentation technique, motivated by style randomization, to further exploit a multi-modal training setup with and without fat suppression. Networks trained with the proposed augmentation are resilient to variations in fat content and show improved robustness compared to training with multi-modal data alone.

  4. MLRC
    Reproducibility Study of "Quantifying Societal Bias Amplification in Image Captioning"
    Farrukh Baratov*, Göksenin Yüksel*, Darie Petcu*, and Jan Bakker*
    In NeurIPS 2023 Main Hall Poster, ML Reproducibility Challenge 2022
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    We study the reproducibility of "Quantifying Societal Bias Amplification in Image Captioning" by Hirota et al., which proposes the LIC metric for measuring bias amplification. Our reproduced results support all four main claims of the original paper. We also extend the work by evaluating LIC for age bias, finding that only a subset of the models amplifies age bias.

Under Review

  1. Under review
    SpeechJEPA: Clustering-Free Latent Prediction for Compute-Efficient Speech Representation Learning
    Göksenin Yüksel, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    Under review at ICASSP 2027
    Abs Code

    Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) were reported to collapse on speech unless anchored by explicit input-level clustering. SpeechJEPA is a clustering-free JEPA that learns speech representations entirely in latent space, without discrete targets, negative samples or auxiliary grounding. A mask-conditioned convolutional predictor regresses several target groups from a single shared context encoding, so the encoder runs once per utterance. Pre-trained on LibriSpeech in 13.8 GPU-hours on one H100, SpeechJEPA beats wav2vec 2.0 with one hour of labels at about 17x fewer training FLOPs, and a 64-GPU-hour model outperforms wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT and WavLM at every labeled-data budget.

  2. Under review
    Bearings: Self-Supervised Soundfield Embeddings from First-Order Ambisonics
    Göksenin Yüksel, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    Under review at ICASSP 2027; arXiv:2609.23152
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code

    Audio encoders capture acoustic content well but have no notion of where sounds come from. Bearings learns spatial audio representations without labels by training a masked autoencoder on first-order Ambisonics, with a decoder conditioned on frozen embeddings from single-channel acoustic encoders. The resulting soundfield embeddings can be combined with different frozen acoustic models without retraining either one. Fused with acoustic representations for sound event localization and detection, they raise the location-dependent F-score from below 4 to 50 on TAU-NIGENS 2021 and to 39 on STARSS23.

  3. Under review
    EquiSELD: Efficient training of equivariant sound event localization and detection networks
    Göksenin Yüksel, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    Under review at ICASSP 2027; arXiv:2609.23156
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code

    First-order Ambisonics signals have an exact O(3) symmetry: rotating or reflecting the signal changes the direction of arrival of the sound sources but not the sources themselves. EquiSELD processes Ambisonics as paired streams of O(3)-invariant scalars and equivariant intensity vectors, producing an invariant activity magnitude and an equivariant direction of arrival with a Multi-ACCDOA readout. It outperforms prior equivariant approaches with far fewer parameters and less training time, outperforms non-equivariant SELD networks on simulated scenes, and is competitive on real-world recordings.

  4. Under review
    WavJEPA: Semantic learning unlocks robust audio foundation models for raw waveforms
    Göksenin Yüksel, Pierre Guetschel, Michael Tangermann, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    Under review at IEEE/ACM Transactions on Audio, Speech, and Language Processing; arXiv:2509.23238
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code Website

    Learning audio representations from raw waveforms overcomes key limitations of spectrogram-based audio representation learning, such as the latency of spectrogram computation and the loss of phase information. We propose WavJEPA, a waveform-based version of the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture that learns high-level semantic representations, substantially outperforming state-of-the-art time-domain audio foundation models while requiring considerably fewer computational resources. We also present WavJEPA-Nat, a multi-channel extension trained on simulated naturalistic scenes that is highly robust to reverberation and noise.

Preprints

  1. arXiv
    GRAM: Spatial general-purpose audio representations
    Göksenin Yüksel, Marcel Gerven, and Kiki Heijden
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2506.00934, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF Code

    Most audio foundation models ignore the spatial structure of real-world sound scenes. We propose GRAM, a general-purpose real-world audio model that uses a multi-channel masked autoencoder to efficiently learn spatial audio representations. We evaluate GRAM and other audio foundation models on high-quality simulations of naturalistic spatial acoustic environments and on real-world recordings, and release two complementary benchmark task suites: NatHEAR and RealSELD.

  2. arXiv
    Remining Hard Negatives for Generative Pseudo Labeled Domain Adaptation
    Göksenin Yüksel, David Rau, and Jaap Kamps
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.14434, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Dense retrievers lose much of their effectiveness when the target domain differs from the training data. This work improves Generative Pseudo Labeling (GPL) domain adaptation by re-mining hard negatives, which strengthens the knowledge distillation signal and improves retrieval performance across several benchmark datasets.

  3. arXiv
    Interpretability Analysis of Domain Adapted Dense Retrievers
    Göksenin Yüksel and Jaap Kamps
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.14459, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Dense retrievers are not robust across domains, and it is unclear what domain adaptation changes inside them. Using integrated gradients, this work explains how adaptation shifts a retriever’s behavior on financial and biomedical datasets: adapted models attend more to domain-specific terms such as "hedge" and "corona", showing how attribution methods can expose the mechanisms behind neural retrieval.

  4. arXiv
    On Correlating Factors for Domain Adaptation Performance
    Göksenin Yüksel and Jaap Kamps
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.14466, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    This work examines which factors explain how well dense retrievers adapt to a new domain. Analyzing the similarity between source and target data, it finds that the distribution of generated query types is an important factor, and that generating queries that match the target domain’s query types substantially improves domain adaptation.
